# Pix Android app

Kotlin, XML Views with ViewBinding. minSdk 29, targetSdk 36, compileSdk 36. Open this `android/` folder in Android Studio.

## What is here

This folder holds one Android app with these parts:

- One activity with a navigation graph (`res/navigation/nav_graph.xml`). `PixNavHostFragment` in `ui/ScreenTransitions.kt` hosts the graph and gives every move between screens its transition (Design 2.1).
- The screens of R&S 6, as Fragments and bottom sheets under `ui/`.
- The modules behind the shared contracts of the Design Documentation. `core/AppContainer.kt` creates them ([Shared contracts](#shared-contracts), [Module classes](#module-classes)).

### Camera

The Camera screen asks for the camera permission and shows the rear camera's preview. A button next to the shutter switches to the front camera (selfie mode, FR-1.14). The button shows only on a phone with a front camera. The front camera is a mirror in the preview, the saved photo, the scene photo, and the streamed video (Design 2.6.4).

The screen saves JPEGs to `Pictures/Pix` through MediaStore. A saved image holds only the camera image. The newest photo shows as a thumbnail, and a tap on the thumbnail opens the photo in the system gallery. A failed capture leaves the camera state and the guide state intact.

A pinch on the preview zooms continuously, and a readout shows the applied ratio. The pinch stops at the camera's real minimum and maximum ratios. A tap on the readout steps through the camera's widest ratio below 1×, then 1× and 2×, where the camera has them. The readout has the Zoom in and Zoom out accessibility actions, so a user can zoom without a gesture.

The top bar has a flash button and an aspect-ratio button. A tap on the flash button moves to the next mode: off, auto, or on. Pix hides the flash button on a phone without a flash. The aspect-ratio button opens the ratios in a row under the bar: 9:16, 3:4, and 1:1, named for the way the user holds the phone. The app keeps the chosen ratio for the next start.

The preview fills the width right below the bar. A 1:1 preview shows inside the 4:3 area. A 9:16 preview extends under the controls, which keep one place for every ratio (R&S 6.2).

The zoom row lies over the bottom of the image. It holds the zoom readout and, when they apply, the pose tip button and the guide-edit mode toggle. A tap on the preview focuses and meters at that point. A brightness bar (`ExposureBarView`) then shows next to the focus box. A drag up or down on the bar, or one finger's vertical drag on the preview, changes the brightness until the next tap. Each tap resets the brightness to the base brightness from *Settings*, which is in EV (FR-1.11, Design 2.8).

`Preview`, full-resolution `ImageCapture`, and YUV `ImageAnalysis` share one viewport of the chosen aspect ratio. The viewport is cut from the middle of the sensor's 4:3 image (3:4, 9:16, or 1:1 in portrait). The saved JPEG has the same crop. The preview uses fit-center scaling, and the screen clips it to the frame. At 1:1, the screen does not clip the 4:3 area but darkens the image outside the square (`CameraCompositionView.dimOutside`).

A ratio change waits for any photo save to end, and then rebinds the camera. The camera keeps the zoom, the exposure, and the flash. The screen holds the last frame until the new ratio streams. The held frame moves with the scene to the new ratio's place and then fades into the new stream (Design 2.6.2). `CameraController.status` (Design 2.1) is `IDLE`, `STARTING`, `READY`, or `UNAVAILABLE`. Screens enable capture only at `READY`, and they offer a retry when the camera fails to start or open.

With a guide on the camera, the **guide-edit mode** toggle in the zoom row decides what a touch on the preview does (AD-17). While the mode is on, a touch anywhere inside the camera image edits the guide. One finger drags the guide. Two fingers resize the guide and also move it with their midpoint. While the mode is off, every touch goes to the preview, and a pinch zooms the camera.

The overlay keeps a touch sequence until all fingers lift. The preview container keeps split touch dispatch off, so later fingers stay in the same sequence. Thus the camera never zooms by mistake. The app enables the mode when the user adds a guide. It disables the mode at the shutter, or when the user hides or removes the guide.

Subject view has the same toggle. With the toggle on, its drags and pinches move the photographer's guide ([Real-time session](#real-time-session)). A touch on the letterbox next to the image never edits the guide. The guide's height stays between 0.21 and 2.1 of the frame height, for any starting height (`GuideState.MIN_HEIGHT`, `MAX_HEIGHT`). At least 20% of each guide dimension stays in the frame (Design 2.6.2 covers very wide guides).

`grabFrame()` copies the latest analysis frame before its first suspension. It returns an upright, cropped bitmap, and the caller owns that bitmap. Call it while a screen has the camera bound. The Camera screen's shutter calls it for the scene photo, in `GenerationViewModel.takeScene`. Keep the returned scene for generation retries.

`setFrameSink()` gives YUV frames on a serial analysis thread, with a `mirrored` flag for the front camera. The receiver must obey the crop and rotation metadata and copy what it needs. It must close the proxy before it returns. It must not block on network work.

The camera UI state follows the zoom that the camera applied. It does not assume that a requested zoom succeeded, so remote zoom can use the same controller. During a pinch, the requested ratio accumulates from the ratio where the pinch began. Meanwhile the Camera readout still shows the applied ratio. Asynchronous camera updates do not move the request under the fingers.

Every ratio is relative to the 1× field of view of the bound camera's main lens. That lens is the primary rear camera, or the front camera in selfie mode. The controller prefers native logical-camera zoom below 1×. Without it, the controller can switch to a separately exposed rear ultrawide whose zoom range overlaps the primary camera's range. The minimum depends on the hardware, for example 0.5×, 0.6×, or 1×. The controller never advertises a ratio that the camera does not support.

A lens switch may pause the preview for a short time. A pinch in progress still updates its target through `STARTING`. A lens change requested during a photo save waits until the capture ends. If an ultrawide cannot bind, the controller returns to the primary camera and updates the supported range.

The photographer's viewfinder has a thin 3×3 grid. The grid fits the real image of the chosen frame and excludes the letterbox bars. All nine cells have the same width and height. A horizon bar sits at the center of the middle cell. Its middle segment counter-rotates with the device roll, and two fixed end markers show the horizontal target.

Within ±2°, the bar aligns and turns dark yellow (`#D4AC24`). It leaves that state only beyond ±3°, so it does not flicker. A smoothed gravity sensor drives the bar, with the accelerometer as the fallback. The sensor drives the bar only while the screen is resumed. With no usable sensor, or with the phone pointed almost straight up or down, only the grid stays. These aids do not consume guide gestures, and they are absent from saved photos and streamed video.

#### Camera verification

Run the lint, format, and unit-test commands below and `./gradlew :app:assembleDebug`. Then do these checks on a phone or on an emulator with a camera:

1. Start the app fresh and grant the camera permission. Check that the whole preview shows in the last ratio chosen (3:4 at first). On another start, deny the permission. Grant it through *Open settings*, then return to Pix.
2. Pinch the preview across the hardware range. Check that the zoom is continuous and the readout shows an applied ratio such as 1.7×. Reach either limit and reverse without lifting the fingers. The readout should follow and not jump back as camera observations arrive. With a guide, enable guide-edit mode. The same pinch should resize the guide and not change the zoom. Disable the mode. The pinch should zoom, and the guide should stay where it is. Check the readout's Zoom in and Zoom out TalkBack actions.
3. Tap the shutter many times while a photo saves. Only one capture should be in flight. Then a new image should appear in the Pix album, with a new thumbnail. The notice “Saved without the guide” should show and disappear after 3 s. Open the thumbnail.
4. Open and close the guide sheet and the room-code sheet. Open the gallery. Move the app to the background and then to the foreground. Return from a full-screen destination. After each step, the camera should resume.
5. On the S22 and the S23 Ultra, compare the framing and rotation of the preview, the saved image, and `grabFrame()`. Check that a file-write failure or a camera failure keeps the shutter usable for a retry.
6. With a guide on, switch the ratio 3:4 → 9:16 → 1:1. The preview, the grid, and the guide should follow without a blank frame. A photo in each ratio should have that shape. Step through the flash modes and take a photo with the flash on. Tap near and far objects to focus, and drag the brightness bar after each tap. With a subject connected, the subject's video, grid, and guide should follow each ratio.
7. Check that the grid divides only the camera image into equal thirds. Check this again after a save message changes the preview area. Tilt the phone left and right. The center bar should rotate, then align in dark yellow near level. Point the phone straight down or up. The level bar, which is then ambiguous, should disappear. Check that guide drags and pinches still work through the grid. Check that photos do not contain the aids. Check that the bar resumes after a return from the gallery.
8. Use a phone with native logical zoom below 1× and a phone with a separate rear ultrawide. On each phone, pinch out to the widest field of view. Check the real minimum: 0.5× only where the camera supports it, 0.6× on a 0.6× device, and 1× without a usable ultrawide. Continue the same pinch across 1× in both directions. Also reverse it during a lens switch's `STARTING` state. While the fingers are down, the lens changes only below 0.95× or above 1.05×. Thus a pinch that wavers at 1× does not switch back and forth. The pinch's last value settles on its own lens. A switch shows the last frame, without the spinner, until the new lens streams. Then the latest requested zoom applies. The readout must stay in primary-camera units and must not reset to the ultrawide's native 1×.
9. Take a photo while the pinch crosses the lens boundary, then keep pinching. The photo should finish saving before a queued lens change applies. The lens change must not interrupt the capture. Check the framing of the preview, the saved photo, the analysis, and the live stream at both lens ranges. If an ultrawide bind fails, check that the primary preview recovers and its supported range updates. Also check that zoom and capture still work. Cancel a pinch or pause the screen, and check that the interaction ends cleanly. At a ratio below 1×, check again that the app restores the zoom after a return from the gallery and from the background.
10. Measure the zoom (NFR-19, IT-10) with the commands in the [Testing Documentation](https://github.com/snuhcs-course/swpp-2026-project-team-10/wiki/Testing-Documentation) (2.1.4). They print Markdown tables for the PR: each pinch's frame intervals and drops, the request-to-frame latency, each lens switch, and the shutter.

The `PixTimings` logcat events include `app.start`, `camera.preview`, and the camera capture events. They also include the zoom measurement's events: `zoom.*`, `camera.status`, `preview.stream`, and `preview.still` (Design 2.6.5). An emulator smoke check does not establish the resolution, startup latency, or frame rate of a real device.

**Look.** Every screen uses the dark-only `Theme.Pix` (`res/values/themes.xml`). The design tokens are in `colors.xml`. They are the ground and the surfaces, glass for controls over the preview, and one accent (`#F2C14E`). They also include the live color (`#36D399`), the guide color (`#5EEAD4`), the danger color, and the red of a saved pose's ♥ (`pix_heart`). The type styles (`TextAppearance.Pix.*`) and the component styles (`Widget.Pix.*`) are in `styles.xml`. The component styles cover primary, secondary, text, glass, icon, and segment buttons, chips, sliders, cards, sheets, and dialogs.

The fonts are Manrope, DM Mono, and Pretendard in `res/font` (SIL OFL 1.1, with the licenses in `assets/licenses`). A text style names the alias of its weight, `@font/pix_medium` to `pix_extrabold`, not a font file (see *Language* below). Icons are vector drawables in `res/drawable` (`ic_*`). Their `_small` versions are 16–20 dp, for use next to text.

The app icon is the adaptive icon in `res/mipmap-anydpi`. It shows the shutter P from [`branding/`](../branding/) on a paper tile (`ic_launcher_background`). `ic_launcher_monochrome` serves themed icons. Notifications use `ic_notification`, the small version of the logo.

The team's UI mockups are the visual reference. Where a mockup differs from the built behavior, the behavior wins. Two examples are pinch zoom with a guide-edit mode toggle instead of zoom chips, and the system number keyboard instead of an on-screen keypad.

**Language.** English is in `res/values/strings.xml`, and Korean is in `res/values-ko/strings.xml`. Both files use the same keys in the same order (FR-10.2). Android uses the first of the two languages in the phone's language list. When the list holds neither, Pix is in English. Pix's own *Settings › Language* chooses a language for Pix alone, through `AppLanguage`. On Android 13 and later, *Settings › Apps › Pix › Language* in the phone's settings does the same. A new string gets its Korean in the same PR, or lint fails.

Korean is in 해요체 (사진을 저장했어요). A name in a message takes 님 (`%1$s님이 나갔어요`), and a number takes 에. Thus no particle depends on how the name or the number ends. Only strings without words (ratios, counters, `@%1$s`) are `translatable="false"`.

Every text appearance sets `android:lineBreakWordStyle="phrase"`. Thus on Android 13 and later, Korean lines break between words. Android 10–12 break them inside words. Manrope has no Hangul, so on a phone in Korean every screen uses Pretendard, Latin letters included. `res/font` points the `pix_*` aliases to Manrope, and `res/font-ko` points them to Pretendard.

The app asks the server for screen text in the screens' language. So far, the only such text is the pose tip. `screenLanguage` (`generation/ScreenLanguage.kt`) gives `ko` when `values-ko/bools.xml` sets `screens_in_korean`, and `en` otherwise. `ScreenLanguages` follows the language from the application and from `MainActivity`. On Android 10–12, only the activity gets Pix's own language.

## How to work on it

- **Fakes.** To work before another owner's module is ready, write a fake of its interface. Use the fake in `core/AppContainer.kt`.
- **Contracts.** Change a contract only through a pull request that the affected owners review. Update the Design Documentation in the same change.
- **Libraries.** Versions are in `gradle/libs.versions.toml`. Add the ones your module needs to `app/build.gradle.kts`.
- **Server address.** `BuildConfig.SERVER_URL` depends on the build type. In a release build, it is Server A, `http://swpp26t10.147-46-242-169.sslip.io/`, which runs `main` and answers only on the campus network. In a debug build, it defaults to the AWS server, `http://3.105.185.251/`. The AWS server is permanent and runs `dev`. Unlike the course servers, it answers from any network, cellular data included. To use another server, put its address in `local.properties` as `pix.serverUrl=...`, or pass `-Ppix.serverUrl=...` to Gradle. Git does not track `local.properties`. Use the laptop's address on the test Wi-Fi, such as `http://192.168.0.10:8000/`. From the emulator, use `http://10.0.2.2:8000/`, the host machine. The servers have no HTTPS, so the app allows plain `http://` and `ws://` traffic. Release builds allow it only to the two course servers (`src/main/res/xml/network_security_config.xml`). Debug builds allow it to any host (`src/debug/res/xml/network_security_config.xml`).

## Real-time session

Two phones connect over the Pix server, with a room code or an invitation. Then they stream phone to phone with WebRTC (Design 1.1.4, 2.5, 2.6.4). The prebuilt library is `io.github.webrtc-sdk:android` (`org.webrtc` package).

- **Run it.** Start the server on the laptop (`server/README.md`), and set `pix.serverUrl` as above. Install the debug build on both phones. Put the laptop and both phones on one Wi-Fi that allows device-to-device traffic. Campus networks usually do not allow it, but a phone hotspot does. The phone that hosts the hotspot can take part too. The peer connection enumerates the network interfaces itself and does not ask Android's network monitor. That monitor does not report the hotspot interface. On one phone, *Shoot together › I'll take the photo › Show a code* shows the code. On the other phone, *Shoot together › I'll be in the photo* joins it.
- **Across networks.** Use the default AWS server, which every network reaches. With a TURN key on the server, each phone also gets the relay (`server/README.md`). To try the relay on one Wi-Fi, build with `-Ppix.relayOnly=true`, or set `pix.relayOnly=true` in `local.properties`. The debug build then uses only the relay. The first session on a phone's mobile data shows "The live view uses mobile data." once on that phone (FR-6.13). When the server refuses a join for too many wrong codes from the network, *See their camera* says so.
- **One phone is enough to test either side.** `server/tools/fake_photographer.py` plays the photographer for Subject view. It sends a test-pattern video, a guide image, guide moves, and style changes. The app ignores `style` since Iter2-P11. The tool echoes zoom, brightness, and flash requests as `camera.state`. It applies a `guide.move` to its guide and echoes `guide.state` with `by: SUBJECT`. `server/tools/fake_subject.py` plays the subject. It connects over WebRTC and receives the video and the guide image, which it checks against the CRC (`--save-guide` keeps the image). It prints every `guide.state` and `camera.state` with the delay since its own request. It sends a zoom, a guide move, a brightness, or a flash request (`--zoom`, `--move cx,cy,height`, `--exposure`, `--flash`, see `server/README.md`).
- **Watch it.** `adb logcat -s PixTimings PixSession PixSignaling PixPeer` shows these lines:
  - The session steps: `room.created`, `peer.joined`, `offer.sent`, `ice.connected`, and `session.connected`.
  - The ping round-trip time (`rtt`) and each ping from the other phone (`ping.received`). Both carry the ping's `ts`, so you can align the logs of the two phones (Design 2.5.1).
  - A `stats` line every 2 s with the codec, frame size, fps, bitrate, and ICE round-trip time from WebRTC's statistics (NFR-5, 6, 12).
  - An `ice.pair` line whenever the candidate pair changes, such as `local=relay/udp remote=srflx network=cellular`. It says whether the video goes direct or through the relay, and over which network (Design 2.6.7, NFR-10).
  - `ice.disconnected` when the link drops. While the link recovers: `session.reconnecting`, `signaling.rejoined`, `ice.restarted`, and `session.reconnected <ms>` (NFR-11).
  - The reason a session ended.
- **States.** `SessionManager.state` follows Design 2.4. When the subject leaves, the photographer closes the peer connection and returns to `Waiting(code)` with the same code. Thus the subject's *Reconnect* can join again. An invitation's session has no code, so the photographer's session ends instead.
- **Reconnecting (#118).** When the link drops while connected, both phones are in `Reconnecting` for up to 10 s (Design 2.6.7, FR-6.12). Each phone opens a new signaling socket. It takes its place again with the rejoin token from `room.created` or `session.joined`. The photographer sends an ICE restart on the same peer connection, so the channels and the video track stay. Subject view shows "Reconnecting…" over the dimmed last frame, and the photographer's live light goes dark. After 10 s, the photographer shows "… disconnected" and returns to `Waiting(code)`. The subject gets `Ended(CONNECTION_LOST)` and the Connection lost screen. Its *Reconnect* takes the subject's place again with the token (`SessionEntry.Rejoin`). To try it, put one phone in airplane mode for 5 s during a session (IT-4, Testing Documentation 2.1.5). The server must support `session.join`.
- **Screens.** Screens draw from `SessionViewModel.state`. They navigate on `SessionViewModel.transitions`, a one-shot stream of state changes, so a return to a screen never replays an old navigation. The "… left" notices come from `SessionViewModel.notices`.
- **Background.** The camera is bound to the Camera screen's view. Thus the app releases the camera whenever another screen or app is in front. Subject view shows "The photographer's camera is paused" after 2 s without frames. If the whole app stays in the background for 60 s during a live session, the photographer's phone ends the session (Design 2.8, Lifecycle).
- **Remote zoom.** While the photographer is connected, `SessionViewModel` runs `RemoteControlHandler`. The handler clamps and applies a `camera.zoom.set` from the subject. It echoes every *applied* zoom as `camera.state`: the subject's request, the photographer's pinch, or accessibility input. Thus both phones end on the same value (FR-7.6). While guide-edit mode is off, Subject view zooms with a pinch on the live video, within the photographer's real range. A pinch updates the readout optimistically, and echoes do not change the request during the pinch. `SubjectViewModel.onZoomGesture` still sends each step as a non-final `camera.zoom.set`, at most every 50 ms (newest wins). When the fingers lift, the pinch is cancelled, or the screen pauses, Subject view sends the last value reliably as `final`. Only a final request makes the photographer's "… set zoom to 2×" notice. The echoes after it settle the readout on the applied ratio. The readout's Zoom in and Zoom out accessibility actions send final requests directly. Timing marks: `zoom.sent` and `zoom.echo` on the subject, and `zoom.received` and `zoom.applied` on the photographer (NFR-7).
- **Remote brightness, flash, and guide moves.** The same handler clamps `camera.exposure.set` to the range and applies it. It applies `camera.flash.set` when the camera has a flash. It applies `guide.move` through the guide repository, which clamps it like the photographer's own gestures. When the camera cannot take a request, the handler answers with the unchanged state. Subject view's brightness button, flash button, and guide-edit mode toggle (shown with a guide) send these requests. Each control follows the finger at once and settles on the echo (`StepSender`, `ExposureScale`). The photographer's phone shows one notice per final action: moved the guide, set brightness to +1, turned the flash on.
- **Verification.** IT-5 and IT-6 in the [Testing Documentation](https://github.com/snuhcs-course/swpp-2026-project-team-10/wiki/Testing-Documentation) (2.1.5) are the scripts for two phones. IT-5 covers guide sync and guide moves from both phones. IT-6 covers remote zoom, brightness, flash, and guide moves. For NFR-7, read `adb logcat -s PixTimings` on the subject. Measure the round trip from `move.sent … final` to `guide.applied`, and from `exposure.sent … final` to `zoom.echo`.
- **Guide sync.** `GuideSyncer` keeps the subject's guide the same as the photographer's (Design 2.5.1, 2.6.1 step 6, 2.6.2).
  - Photographer: when connected, `SessionViewModel` runs `GuideSyncer` as the sender. It sends the smoothed mask as an 8-bit grayscale PNG (`mask-png`, protocol 3), at most 720 px tall. The PNG goes in `guide.image.begin`/`chunk`/`end` messages, as Base64 chunks of at most 12 KiB with a CRC32 at the end. Then the sender sends the current `guide.state`. It skips the image when the subject's `hello` already named that guide id (`PeerInfo.haveGuideId`). It caches the encoded images per guide id.
  - After that, the sender sends every change from `GuideRepository.changes`. Gesture steps go on the realtime channel at most every 50 ms (newest wins). The final value of a gesture goes at once on the reliable channel. A new guide goes as image plus state, and a removed guide goes as `guide.clear`.
  - Subject: `SessionViewModel` runs the receiver from the moment the subject joins, because the image can arrive before this phone's own `Connected`. The receiver rebuilds and checks the image, and derives the outline from the mask (`MaskGuideImageCodec`). It writes the image and the state into the mirror `GuideRepository`. Subject view draws that mirror read-only with `GuideOverlayView`. A state that arrives before its image waits for the image. The session drops a stale `seq`.
  - Reference photo: after a guide's image and state, the photographer also sends its reference photo for Subject view's thumbnail (FR-3.9). The photo is a JPEG at most 512 px on its long side, in `guide.reference.begin`/`chunk`/`end`. The photographer sends it once per guide, and skips it when the subject's `hello` names it in `haveReferenceId`. The subject keeps it in memory only (`MirrorReference`). It shows the photo in a corner like the photographer's thumbnail, and each phone keeps its own corner.
  - Timing marks: `guide.sent` and `guide.applied` in `PixTimings`, also for the reference. `PixGuideSync` logs a dropped image or reference and the reason.
  - From the subject: a guide chosen on Subject view goes the other way (Design 2.6.5). `GuideSyncer.offer` draws it on the subject's phone at once and sends it with its `anchor` and `source`. The photographer adopts it as its own guide, does not send the image back, and shows "… set a pose guide". Without the photographer's answer within 5 s, the subject shows "Couldn't send the guide".
  - The photographer's images also carry `anchor` and `source`. Thus *Save guide* on Subject view keeps the guide as the photographer's phone does (FR-6.16), with the reference copy as its thumbnail.
- **Generate poses on both phones.** In a session, *Generate poses* is on the subject's phone too. `GenerationViewModel` reports each description to `DescriptionSyncer` (`DescriptionShare`). Its sender sends the description in `pose.description`: `OPEN` with the text and `writer`, then `STARTED` or `CLOSED`. It also sends a JPEG copy of the scene photo once, in `pose.scene.*` (Design 2.5.1, 2.6.5). One field at a time holds the description (`DescriptionForm`). A field holds it from a tap on the field until Done, the keyboard closing, a button, or leaving the screen. `GenerationViewModel.descriptionWriter` decides which phone's field holds it. Meanwhile the other phone's field, chips, and buttons wait with "… is typing…". The subject's typing goes as `pose.description.edit`. Its tap of the button goes as `pose.description.go`, with `suggest` for an empty field. This starts the set like the photographer's own tap. Timing marks: `pose.sent description …` and `pose.applied description …` in `PixTimings`. `PixDescriptionSync` logs a refused photo.
- **Pick a pose on both phones.** Only the photographer's phone generates poses. `GenerationViewModel` reports each set to `PoseSyncer` (`PoseShare`). Its sender sends the set's step in `pose.step`, and then `PICKING` or the end. It sends the planned slots and each failed candidate (`pose.planned`, `pose.candidate.failed`, Iter3-A13). It sends a JPEG copy of each candidate as the candidate arrives, at most 1024 px, in `pose.candidate.*` (Design 2.5.1, 2.6.5). Subject view says what the photographer is doing. It opens one screen of each set by itself: the subject's *Generating poses* or *Pick a pose*. The subject's *Generating poses* (`SubjectGeneratingFragment`) shows the same slots in the same order and continues to *Pick a pose* by itself. Its *Use this pose* sends `pose.pick`. The photographer's phone takes the pick only if no phone chose before it. It then continues as with its own tap, to Reference confirm with "… chose this pose". The set stays on the subject's phone while the link reconnects. When the link is back, the photographer sends the set again, or only its end if the set ended in the meantime. Timing marks: `pose.sent` and `pose.applied` in `PixTimings`. `PixPoseSync` logs a refused copy.
- **Reference confirm on both phones.** Both phones also share the chosen pose's Reference confirm. `ReferenceViewModel.onCandidatePicked` opens a confirm in `ConfirmSyncer` (`ConfirmShare`). Its sender sends the confirm's step (`FINDING`, `READY`, `USED`, `CLOSED`) in `guide.confirm`. At `READY`, it also sends the outline's mask as `mask-png` in `guide.confirm.image.*` (Design 2.5.1, 2.6.5). Only the photographer's phone finds the person. The app does not share a photo from *Add a pose guide*. Subject view opens the subject's *Your reference* once per confirm and shows "… is checking the guide…". Its *Use this guide* sends `guide.confirm.use`. `ReferenceViewModel` takes it only while that outline is on screen. It then sets the guide as for its own tap and returns to the camera with "… used this guide". Like the set, the confirm stays through a reconnect. Timing marks: `guide.sent confirm …` and `guide.applied confirm …` in `PixTimings`. `PixConfirmSync` logs a refused outline.
- **Photos on both phones.** Each photo taken in a session also goes to the subject's "Pix" album. `CameraViewModel` reports the photo to `PhotoSyncer` (`PhotoShare`). The syncer sends the saved JPEG itself in chunks, one photo at a time. Each chunk goes through `SessionManager.sendBulk`, behind every other reliable message. At most 32 chunks go unacknowledged (`photo.ack`), because the message buffers on the receiving phone drop what does not fit. The subject writes the chunks to a file in the cache and saves the file through `PhotoSaver` (`PhotoAlbum`). `SessionViewModel` runs the syncer for the whole session. Thus a photo continues after a short drop and does not start again. When a session ends, the syncer drops what remains, and the Camera screen says "Couldn't send a photo to …" (Design 2.5.1, 2.6.5, 2.6.7).
- **Camera frames.** `SessionViewModel` sets `CameraFrameSource` as the frame sink from the moment a subject starts to connect. `CameraFrameSource` obeys `cropRect` and `rotationDegrees`, and it flips the front camera's frames. The `frames` line in the `PixTimings` log counts the frames that go to WebRTC.
- **Tests.** `app/src/test/.../session/` covers the envelope codec, the channel table, and the signaling JSON against the server's examples. It also covers the NV21 packing for every plane layout and the guide image chunks. It tests the session state machine, reconnection included, with fake signaling and peer clients (`SessionFakes.kt`). It tests the mask codec with real bitmaps (Robolectric). It tests `GuideSyncer` and `PoseSyncer` on both sides with fake codecs (`ModuleFakes.kt`), and also `ConfirmSyncer`, `DescriptionSyncer`, and `PhotoSyncer`. `guide/MaskPngTest` covers the mask PNG round trip (`MaskPng`, plain JVM). A manager test that ends while connected must call `leave`. Otherwise the virtual-time ping loop keeps `runTest` from finishing.

## Pose generation

*Guide › Generate poses here* lets the user take a scene photo. The user then describes a pose or lets the server suggest four. The app sends the request through the Pix server and shows up to four pose candidates. The chosen candidate goes to Reference confirm like an uploaded photo (R&S F4, Design 2.5.3, 2.6.3).

- **Run it.** A server needs an OpenRouter key for pose generation (`server/README.md`). For a laptop server, start it with that key and set `pix.serverUrl` as above. An emulator reaches the laptop only when `pix.serverUrl` is `http://10.0.2.2:8000/`. The service bills each set as up to four images.
- **Scene photo.** The user takes the photo on the Camera screen. The camera keeps running, so zoom and the grid work as usual. While `GenerationUiState.phase` is `FRAMING`, `CameraFragment` shows "Take the scene photo" with *Cancel*. It hides *Guide*, *Shoot together*, the gear, the thumbnail, and any earlier guide. The flash button controls the phone's light for the photo instead (FR-4.1). The shutter takes the frame with `grabFrame()` and saves nothing. In `REVIEWING`, the screen shows the photo over the preview with *Use this photo* and *Shoot again*. *Use this photo* works only after `PersonDetector` finds a person in the photo (FR-4.13). When the check cannot run, the photo can still be used. The app sends nothing before *Use this photo* (FR-4.1). `CameraFragment.renderScenePhoto` holds this logic and runs last in `render`. `AndroidPoseImageCodec` prepares the confirmed photo on the phone: long side at most 1024 px, JPEG quality 85, no EXIF. The server checks the photo and rejects any other kind. It does not resize the photo (Design 2.6.3, step 1).
- **Generate poses.** After *Use this photo*, `GenerationUiState.phase` is `DESCRIBING`. `DescribePoseFragment` shows the description field, which takes 100 characters and has a counter. It shows the descriptions used before as chips, each with a ✕ (`PromptHistory`, FR-4.11). In an invitation's session, a switch shows only the ones used with that friend. A ✕ empties the field. One button reads *Suggest 4 poses* while the field is empty and *Generate 4 poses from this* when it holds a description (Iter3-A13). The app sends nothing before the user taps it. `DescriptionForm` holds the form apart from the fragment, so `DescriptionFormTest` runs it on the real layout. `PoseRequest.of` makes a blank field a request for suggestions and folds whitespace like the server. When the service refuses every pose of a description, *Couldn't create poses* asks for a different description or another photo. It asks for either, because the service does not say which of the two it refused. *Edit description* returns with the text kept, and *Back to camera* leads to another photo (US-5). *Try other poses* on Pick a pose also returns here with the photo and the description kept (`GenerationViewModel.describeAgain`). Thus the next set can ask for something else.
- **Requests.** `RemotePoseGenerator` encodes the scene once. It first sends `POST /pose-suggestions`, either for suggestions or with the description for three variations of it. The generator sends the description's own `POST /poses` at the same time, so its candidate does not wait. When the variations do not arrive within 10 s (`VARIATION_TIMEOUT_MS`), or at all, the generator sends the description again in their place. It emits `GenerationEvent.Planned` with the four poses (`pose-1` … `pose-4`, the description first). Then it sends one `POST /poses` per remaining pose, in parallel. Each request has the pose as its `description` (a UTF-8 text part) and its own seed. The generator emits a `CandidateEvent` as each request ends, never before `Planned`. A request without an answer after 60 s becomes `TIMEOUT`. The HTTP client in `AppContainer` waits 65 s, so this limit decides. OkHttp's default of 10 s would end every generation. `GenerationViewModel` puts the same 60 s limit on a whole run, the suggestions or variations included. At that limit, it counts the poses still unanswered as timed out, so Generating poses never lasts longer. Cancelling the collector closes the connections, and the server then stops its own calls to the image service.
- **Consent.** The notice appears before the first scene photo (FR-4.2). The app keeps the answer in DataStore (`pose_generation`), so the notice does not appear again after an agreement. *Not now* sends nothing and stays on the sheet. To see the notice again, withdraw the agreement in *Settings › AI pose generation*, or clear the app's data.
- **Screens.** While the user takes the scene photo, system Back is *Shoot again* when the photo shows, and *Cancel* before that. Generating poses moves to Pick a pose when at least one candidate arrived, and to Couldn't create poses otherwise. Pick a pose omits a pose that failed. *Use this pose* gives the chosen image to Reference confirm and then discards the scene photo and the other candidates. The ♥ on a candidate cuts the person from the candidate and saves the result to the library as an AI pose (FR-4.14). It does this on either phone, for that phone's library only. *Use this pose* on a saved candidate continues with that saved guide. On Generate poses and Generating poses, system Back does what *Cancel* does. On Couldn't create poses, it does what *Back to camera* does. Both stop the requests and discard the scene photo and the candidates. On Pick a pose, system Back is *Try other poses*. The ✕ at the top returns to the camera and discards the photo, the description, and the candidates.
- **Watch it.** `adb logcat -s PixTimings PixPoses` shows these lines:
  - `pose.person found` or `pose.person none`: the person check of the scene photo (FR-4.13).
  - `pose.start described` or `pose.start suggested`.
  - `pose.planned`: the app knows the poses.
  - `pose.first`, then `pose.done 3 of 4 ready` (NFR-4).
  - One `PixPoses` line for each failed request, with the server's error code and message.
- **Tests.** `generation/RemotePoseGeneratorTest` uses a fake `PixApi`. It covers a description with and without its variations, suggestions, the parallel requests, the seeds, the error mapping, the timeouts, and cancellation. `ui/generation/GenerationViewModelTest` covers taking and confirming the photo, *Generate poses*, the phases, and a rejected description. It also covers partial failure, *Try other poses*, retry, cancel, selection, and consent. `ui/generation/DescriptionFormTest` (Robolectric) covers the form.

## Accounts and friends

*I'll take the photo* on the *Shoot together* sheet opens *Share my camera* (`FriendsFragment`). For a signed-out user, the screen offers *Sign in to add friends* in place of the list. For a signed-in user, it lists the friends with their online status, and the received requests. *Sent requests · N* opens the requests the user sent, each with *Withdraw*. *Show a code* stays at the bottom in both cases, because room codes need no account (R&S F5, 6.4, 6.7, and Design 2.1, 2.5.3). *Sign in* returns to the screen that opened it: *Share my camera*, *Invitation*, or Settings. After *Sign up* or *Pix ID setup*, the app also returns to that screen. *Check* on Sign up and Pix ID setup asks `POST /auth/check-pix-id` whether the Pix ID is free. Thus it needs a server with that endpoint.

- **Run it.** Use a server with the accounts endpoints: the default AWS server, Server B, or a laptop as above. Sign-up and sign-in with a Pix ID and a password work everywhere. Google sign-in needs three more things. The server needs `PIX_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`. `app/build.gradle.kts` needs the web client ID. The team's Google Cloud project needs your debug key. To register the key, run `./gradlew :app:signingReport`. Then ask a project owner to add the `SHA1` of `Variant: debug` as an Android OAuth client for `com.lastpenguin.pix`. Without the key, Google's sheet fails and the app says so. An emulator needs a Google Play image signed in to a test user.
- **Tokens.** `AuthTokens` keeps the account and its tokens in DataStore (`account`). The backup rules exclude this store from backups and device transfers (`res/xml/data_extraction_rules.xml`). `AuthTokens` runs one refresh at a time for every caller. Each refresh spends its refresh token, and a spent token ends the session when the app sends it again. `BearerTokens` adds the access token to `PixAccountApi` calls. It answers `401 UNAUTHENTICATED` with one refresh and one retry. The three REST clients in `AppContainer` each have their own dispatcher. Thus a refresh on the `PixAuthApi` client never waits in a queue behind the calls that wait for it. A refused refresh signs the phone out, and *Share my camera* says so.
- **Presence.** While a user is signed in and Pix is in front, `PresenceConnection` holds one signaling socket. It opens the socket with the access token, which marks the user online for friends (Design 2.5.2). It retries after 1 s, doubles the wait up to 30 s, and closes the socket in the background. A session keeps its own socket, also opened with the token, so friends see "In a session". While *Share my camera* or *Sent requests* is on screen, it asks for the lists every 10 s, with or without pushes.
- **Invitations (#119).** *Invite* on *Share my camera* calls `SessionViewModel.invite`. `POST /invitations` opens the session on the server with the photographer's place. The phone takes that place with `SessionEntry.Invite` and waits in `Inviting` on *Invite sent*. The friend gets a notification on the phone, or opens the invite link's page. A tap on either opens `pix://invite/{id}` (*Invitation*). *Accept* gives the friend's place, which `SessionEntry.Invitation` takes (Design 2.6.6). A decline, or no answer in 60 s, opens *Invite not accepted*. Friend requests arrive as notifications that open *Share my camera* (`pix://friends`). From Android 13, a notification needs the user's permission, and *Share my camera* asks for it once per run. An invitation whose notification the user dismissed still waits for its minute on *See their camera*. That screen lists the received invitations with *Accept* and *Decline* (`IncomingInvitationsViewModel`, #188). The list comes from `GET /invitations/incoming` every 5 s while that screen or *Who takes the photo?* is on screen. Thus it needs a server with that endpoint. *I'll be in the photo* shows how many invitations wait.
- **Push setup.** `app/google-services.json` holds the app's Firebase project (`pix-swpp`), and the `google-services` Gradle plugin reads it. The repository includes this file, because it is not a secret, unlike the server's service account key (`server/README.md`, *Invitations and push*). The manifest disables Firebase's start-up registration. Instead, `PushTokenRegistrar` asks for the FCM token after each sign-in and registers it with `POST /devices`. Thus a phone that nobody signed in to gets no pushes. An emulator with a Google APIs image also receives pushes. To open an invitation without a push to a second phone, open its link: `adb shell am start -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d http://<server>/i/<id>`.
- **Watch it.** `adb logcat -s PixAccount PixPresence PixPush` shows a refused refresh, Google sign-in failures, "Online" when the presence socket opens, and a failed push registration. `PixSession` shows an invitation's `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED`.
- **Tests.** `account/` covers the rules, the tokens and their refresh, and the repositories with fakes of the REST interfaces (`AccountFakes.kt`). It also covers presence in virtual time and the account client against OkHttp's MockWebServer (`BearerTokensTest`). `ui/account/` covers both ViewModels. Under Robolectric, it also covers *Share my camera* (`FriendsFragment`), Sent requests, and the forms on their real layouts.

## Lint, format, and test

Run these commands from `android/` before you push. On every pull request, the `android-lint` workflow runs `spotlessCheck` and `lintDebug`. The `android-test` workflow runs `testDebugUnitTest`.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `./gradlew spotlessApply` | Formats Kotlin and Gradle files with ktlint. |
| `./gradlew spotlessCheck` | Fails if a file is not formatted or breaks a ktlint rule. |
| `./gradlew :app:lintDebug` | Android Lint. Errors fail the run. Warnings only appear in `app/build/reports/lint-results-debug.html`. |
| `./gradlew :app:testDebugUnitTest` | All unit tests, in `app/src/test/`. They run on the JVM, without a phone or an emulator. They need JDK 21 or later (Robolectric's Android 36 sandbox). Guide overlay tests check Canvas rendering and touch dispatch, with drag and pinch transitions and hit testing outside the guide. Pinch and slider tests cover touch order, late observed updates, the final value, invalid ranges, accessibility, and keyboard input. The guide-edit mode test checks that one pinch zooms or edits the guide by the mode alone. Screen tests start every real Fragment and bottom sheet with `FragmentScenario` on a container of fakes (`ui/TestAppContainer`, `ui/ScreenTestSupport`). |
| `./gradlew :app:jacocoTestReport` | Runs `testDebugUnitTest` with coverage. The line and branch coverage is in `app/build/reports/jacoco/jacocoTestReport/html/index.html`. The exclusions and targets are in the [Testing Documentation](https://github.com/snuhcs-course/swpp-2026-project-team-10/wiki/Testing-Documentation) (2.1.2–2.1.4). |

- **Rules.** ktlint rules are in `.editorconfig`, which Android Studio's formatter also reads. After you change that file, run `./gradlew clean` once, or Spotless keeps the old rules.
- **Suppressing.** For one place, use `@Suppress("ktlint:standard:<rule>")` or `@SuppressLint("<LintId>")` with a comment that says why. To disable an Android Lint check for the whole app, add it to `lint { disable += ... }` in `app/build.gradle.kts`.
- **Tests.** Put a test in the same package as its class, under `app/src/test/java/`. `app/src/androidTest/` holds only `PersonCheckEval`, a measurement of the phone's person check for the AI module tests. It runs on a device and never in CI (Testing Documentation 3.1). Add tests in the pull request that writes the code. Plain JVM tests see only Android stubs (`isReturnDefaultValues`), so keep coordinate and state logic in plain Kotlin. When a test needs real Views, Canvas, touch events, or Material widgets, run it with `@RunWith(AndroidJUnit4::class)` (Robolectric). Also add `@GraphicsMode(GraphicsMode.Mode.NATIVE)` if it draws or reads pixels. These tests use synthetic guide bitmaps and need no camera permission, segmentation, or server.
