# Android context

- CameraX is the sole camera owner. Bind it to the Camera screen's view lifecycle, not the activity. Preview, capture, and analysis share one viewport of the chosen aspect ratio (3:4, 9:16, or 1:1 in portrait, cut from the middle of the sensor's 4:3), and ImageCapture saves that crop. PreviewView uses FIT_CENTER but still draws the camera image around the crop, so the Camera screen clips it to the frame. Saved photos never include View overlays.
- `CameraController.status` (Design 2.1) reports asynchronous startup and failure: READY requires an open camera and an analysis frame.
- CameraX starts every binding without the exposure index or the flash mode, so `CameraXController` keeps them, the ratio, and the zoom, and applies them again after a rebind, a lens switch, or a lifecycle stop, staying STARTING until the exposure is back; a ratio change rebinds only once no photo is being saved. While a lens without a flash is bound, the capabilities report no flash and the state reports it off. `CameraController.state` holds the applied values, and every `camera.state` carries all of them (`RemoteControlHandler` also sends one when exposure, flash, or ratio change).
- Call grabFrame before navigation stops the camera: it snapshots cached YUV data before suspending, then converts the caller-owned image off the main thread. FrameSink runs on the serial analysis executor and must copy/crop/rotate as needed and close ImageProxy before returning; never retain camera buffers or wait on the network.
- Camera's ratio readout renders observed zoom; remote zoom must echo that same applied state. While a pinch is in progress, asynchronous observations must not move the requested ratio under the fingers. Subject view shows the requested ratio optimistically during its pinch and settles on the applied echo afterward. Camera status is separate from whether the preview surface has displayed a frame; use camera.preview timing for visible startup.
- All camera ratios use primary-rear-camera units, including capabilities and remote echo. Prefer native logical-camera zoom; a separately exposed ultrawide is eligible only if its normalized range overlaps the primary range. Preserve the real hardware minimum, continue an existing pinch through STARTING during lens switching, and defer lens changes until an in-flight capture finishes.
- Camera and Subject view zoom with a pinch on the preview or the live video (`PinchZoomController`, AD-17); there is no zoom slider, and the ratio readout keeps the Zoom in / Zoom out accessibility actions. Subject view's requests (zoom, brightness, guide moves) go through `StepSender`: the first step at once, then at most every 50 ms with the newest winning, and the final value at once on release, cancellation, or pause; accessibility changes are final requests. While a sender is `adjusting`, the screen shows its own value and the photographer's echoes wait; the first echo after the final value wins. `ExposureScale` is the brightness slider's scale on both screens. CameraCapabilities.zoomStops remains in the contract for later controls. Use `TrackedSliderController` for every Material slider (guide opacity, brightness): it keeps the thumb under the user during a drag and commits exactly one final value; it takes positive values only, so the brightness slider runs from 1 and maps to exposure indexes. `PinchZoomController` owns the preview's touches, so tap to focus comes through its `onTap`.
- Guide-edit mode (`guideEditing` in each screen's UI state, never synchronized) decides who owns the preview's touches: with it on, `GuideOverlayView.editable` is true, every touch starting inside the camera image edits the guide until all fingers lift, and the `PinchZoomController` is disabled; with it off, the overlay passes everything through and the pinch zooms. The Camera screen turns the mode on when a guide is added and off on the shutter or when the guide is hidden or removed; a hidden guide cannot be edited on either screen. Subject view starts with it off and shows its toggle while a guide is on the video; its drags and pinches are applied on the subject's phone at once, clamped with `GuideGeometry` for the photographer's frame, and sent as `guide.move`; the mirror's echoes are held until the gesture ends, except `visible` and `opacity`, which stay the photographer's. On the photographer, `RemoteControlHandler` drops a move without a visible guide or for a guide it has since replaced (the move names its guide) and otherwise applies it through the guide repository, and `GuideSyncer.attribute(SUBJECT)` makes the echoed `guide.state` say `by: SUBJECT` until its final state is sent. Keep splitMotionEvents disabled on the preview container to preserve a single guide touch sequence as fingers join or leave.
- Frame coordinates are relative to the chosen frame (AD-10): `GuideOverlayView`, `CameraCompositionView`, and `InMemoryGuideRepository` take its width / height: the photographer's from `CameraController.state`, and a ratio change clamps the guide again with a final update. A new guide starts at `ReferenceGuide.anchor` (`GuideGeometry.start`), and the height limits stay 0.21–2.1 of the frame height for every start, so the subject's mirror, which has no anchor, clamps alike. The subject's mirror clamps with `NARROWEST_FRAME_ASPECT` (9:16), whose limits contain every ratio's, so a `guide.state` that arrives before its `camera.state` is never moved; the subject's overlay and grid follow `SessionManager.peerCamera`. Subject view's `SurfaceViewRenderer` stays `wrap_content` and centered, so it measures itself to the video like the overlays fit the frame; at `match_parent` it crops every frame to the view's shape even with `SCALE_ASPECT_FIT`. Its pinch is on the whole video area (`videoContainer`), the margins included.
- CameraCompositionView draws only inside the fit-centered image of the frame; it is non-clickable so camera/guide gestures pass through. Its view-scoped CameraLevelMonitor starts on resume and stops on pause, with gravity/accelerometer fallback and no shared camera or session state. Grid and level aids never enter ImageCapture or ImageAnalysis.
- Run ./gradlew spotlessCheck :app:lintDebug :app:testDebugUnitTest :app:assembleDebug from android/. All tests live in `app/src/test/` (no `androidTest`): pure logic uses plain JVM tests, and View/Canvas/touch/Material tests use Robolectric via `AndroidJUnit4` with native graphics. Robolectric's SDK 36 sandbox needs JDK 21+; Android Studio's JBR works. Permissions, real CameraX capture, MediaStore, and hardware resolution still require an emulator or phone.
- The UI is the dark-only `Theme.Pix`: build screens from the tokens in `res/values/colors.xml` and the `TextAppearance.Pix.*` / `Widget.Pix.*` styles in `styles.xml`, not raw colors or Material defaults ([README.md](README.md), Look). Each text appearance names its font weight's file; never set `android:fontFamily` in the theme, because a theme-level value overrides every text appearance's font. Two-way switches are `Widget.Pix.Button.Segment` buttons selected with `View.isSelected`.
- To render screens in a Robolectric test, inflate through a started `AppCompatActivity` (`Robolectric.buildActivity(...).setup()`) and attach the view to its window. Otherwise `<Button>` stays a platform Button, `app:` attributes are ignored, and start drawables and button icons are not laid out.
- Open `android/` in Android Studio. Commands for formatting, lint, unit tests, and the build are in [README.md](README.md); run them from `android/` before pushing. If `java` is not on the PATH, set `JAVA_HOME` to Android Studio's bundled JBR.
- The shared contracts listed in [README.md](README.md) change only through a reviewed pull request together with the Design Documentation. Module classes behind them belong to the issue owner named in each file's `Owner:` comment; keep other owners' `TODO(#n)` markers in place.
- `core/AppContainer.kt` is the only place modules are created (`AppContainer.create(context)`), and `ui/PixViewModels.kt` the only place ViewModels get them. Replace a module with a fake there, never in a screen. A screen test builds an `AppContainer` from the test fakes (`ui/TestAppContainer` in `app/src/test/`) and installs it with `PixApplication.installContainer` before `launchFragmentInContainer`, so the real factory hands the Fragment fake modules; grant the camera permission through Robolectric's shadow and run the main looper (`shadowOf(getMainLooper()).idle()`) after each state change or click, because the ViewModels collect on `Dispatchers.Main`; a loop with `delay` needs `idleFor`. A screen with WebRTC's `SurfaceViewRenderer` needs `@Config(shadows = [ShadowSurfaceViewRenderer::class], instrumentedPackages = ["org.webrtc"])`, because `init` would create an EGL context. Navigation is checked with `attachNav` (`ui/ScreenTestSupport`), a `TestNavHostController` on the app's graph. Set a `MaterialButton`'s `isChecked` before `isEnabled`: it ignores `setChecked` while disabled.
- `BuildConfig.SERVER_URL` is set per build type in `app/build.gradle.kts`: release builds use Server A, and debug builds use `pix.serverUrl` from `local.properties` or a Gradle property, defaulting to the emulator's host address. The course servers have no TLS, so cleartext is allowed through the network security config, not through `usesCleartextTraffic`, which Android ignores once a config exists: `src/main/res/xml/network_security_config.xml` lists the two course hosts for release builds, and the file of the same name under `src/debug/res/xml/` allows every host for debug builds. A new server host must be added to the main file.
- Session code (`session/`) touches its state only on `RtcSessionManager`'s serial scope; WebRTC and OkHttp callbacks only publish events. Never call `dispose()` from a WebRTC callback thread, and dispose the peer connection before the video source; it disposes the tracks itself.
- Screens render from `SessionViewModel.state` and navigate from `SessionViewModel.transitions` (one-shot). After the subject leaves, the photographer is back in `Waiting(code)`, not `Ended`; the subject gets `Ended(CONNECTION_LOST)` and reconnects with the same code. `SessionViewModel.lastCode` is kept only for Session not found (editing) and Connection lost (Reconnect, and another try after a failed one); Leave, the photographer's End session, a room gone while reconnecting, and Start as the photographer clear it, and `JoinCodeFragment` offers it only when opened with the `keepCode` argument from Session not found (#81).
- The camera binds to the Camera screen's view lifecycle and streams frames through `CameraController.setFrameSink` from `Connecting` on; leaving the screen pauses the subject's video, and 60 s of the whole app in the background ends a live session (`ProcessLifecycleOwner` in `SessionViewModel`).
- Guide sync (`session/GuideSyncer.kt`): the photographer sends while `Connected`; the subject receives from `Connecting` on, because `SessionManager.incoming` does not replay and the image can arrive before the subject's own `Connected`. The subject's `hello` carries the mirror's guide id so an image it already has is not resent. The guide image is the cutout only (WebP with alpha, ≤ 720 px tall); the subject rebuilds the outline with `OutlineExtractor`, and nothing draws the cutout, because the guide is an outline only. No `anchor` is sent: the subject's guide takes its place from the `guide.state` that always follows the image. Bitmap work stays behind `GuideImageCodec` so syncer tests run on the plain JVM.
- Guide outline (Design 2.6.1): `MlKitReferenceGuideMaker` smooths the confidence mask once (`MaskSmoothing.kt`) and puts the result in the cutout's alpha; `OutlineExtractor` only traces that alpha's 0.5 level (`Contour.kt`) and draws it with an anti-aliased stroke. The subject outlines the received cutout the same way, so both phones draw the same line; never smooth again on the subject or inside `OutlineExtractor`.
- Pose generation prepares the scene photo on the phone (`AndroidPoseImageCodec`: long side at most 1024 px, JPEG 85, no EXIF); the server rejects anything else and never resizes. The scene photo is a step of the Camera screen, not a screen of its own: while `GenerationUiState.phase` is `FRAMING` or `REVIEWING`, `CameraFragment.renderScenePhoto` runs last in `render` and overrides the camera's own controls, and the shutter calls `GenerationViewModel.takeScene` (`grabFrame`) instead of saving; nothing is sent before *Generate 4 poses* or *Skip* on the screen after *Use this photo*. The pose API's OkHttp client must wait longer than `RemotePoseGenerator.TIMEOUT_MS`; OkHttp's 10 s read timeout would end every generation. *Use this photo* leads to Generate poses (`DESCRIBING`, `DescribePoseFragment`), where the user describes a pose or skips; a blank description, like *Skip*, asks the server for suggested poses (`PoseRequest.Suggested`); a description asks it for three variations, while the description's own request is already on its way; `PoseGenerator.generate` reports the poses (`GenerationEvent.Planned`) before any candidate, so `RemotePoseGenerator` holds back an early candidate until then. The generation screens navigate from `GenerationUiState.phase` and only while they are the current destination. `RemotePoseGenerator` takes its image codec and dispatcher as parameters so its tests run on the JVM.
- To drive the app on an emulator from the shell, `adb shell uiautomator dump --windows` is needed to see bottom sheets and dialogs; the plain dump often returns only the activity's window.
- JVM unit tests use `isReturnDefaultValues`, so `Log` and `SystemClock` are safe to call, but framework objects such as `Uri.parse(...)` come back null; a test that needs a real `Uri` runs under Robolectric. A session test that ends while connected must call `leave`, because the 2 s ping loop never lets `runTest` drain virtual time.
